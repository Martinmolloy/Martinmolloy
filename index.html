Who we work with

Built around how your sector uses power

Solar economics come down to one thing: using the power while your roof is producing it. These are the sectors where the numbers work hardest.

Logistics and warehousing

Large, uncluttered roofs over daytime operations: the ideal solar site. Run sorting, cold storage and dock charging on your own power, and turn an idle roof into a cost hedge.

Manufacturing

Steady daytime load is the best match solar can get. Offset production-hour consumption at a fixed rate and take energy volatility out of your unit economics.

Agriculture and food

Refrigeration, milking and processing run through daylight hours. Capex buyers may qualify for TAMS support; on a PPA it's €0 upfront either way.

Retail

Trading hours track the sun. Lighting, refrigeration and HVAC draw hardest exactly when the roof produces most, so self-consumption stays high all year.

Hospitality and leisure

Kitchens, laundry, pools and summer occupancy peak with the sun. Cut one of your biggest controllable costs without touching the guest experience.

Offices and healthcare

Year-round daytime baseload from IT, ventilation and equipment. Predictable consumption makes for a precise system design and a dependable saving.

Common questions

Everything businesses ask before switching

Straight answers to the questions Irish business owners ask us most. Don't see yours? Ask the team directly.

What exactly is a Solar PPA?
A Power Purchase Agreement is solar as a service. Aura Energy funds, installs, owns and operates a solar system on your roof at no cost to you. You simply agree to buy the electricity it produces at a fixed rate per kWh, set below what you currently pay the grid. You save money from day one without spending a cent, and we take care of everything else.
How much cheaper is the PPA rate than my grid rate?
Typically up to 30% below your current unit rate, depending on your site, roof, consumption profile and the term you choose. Because the rate is fixed, the gap usually widens over time as grid prices rise. We'll quote your exact rate after a free site assessment, with a clear side-by-side of what you pay now versus what you'd pay under the PPA.
Who owns the solar panels?
Under the PPA, Aura Energy owns and is fully responsible for the system during the agreement, including insurance, maintenance and performance. At the end of the term, ownership transfers to your business. If you'd rather own from day one, choose our capex option and the system is yours the moment it's commissioned.
What happens at the end of the PPA term?
The system transfers to your business, and from then on every kWh it generates is free. Solar panels carry 25-year performance warranties and typically keep producing well beyond that, so you'll enjoy many years of zero-cost power after the term ends. You can also buy out the system early at any point during the agreement.
What if the system underperforms or breaks down?
That's our problem, not yours. It's the core of the PPA model. You only pay for electricity the system actually produces. We monitor every system remotely, and our smart monitoring flags issues automatically, often before anyone on-site notices. Repairs, replacements and insurance are all covered by us for the full term.
Does a PPA affect my balance sheet or borrowing?
A PPA is an operating arrangement, not a loan or a lease of equipment you own, so there's no debt on your balance sheet and no capex approval needed. You're simply buying electricity, the same as you do today, just cheaper. (As with any contract, your accountant can confirm treatment for your specific circumstances.)
Should I choose the PPA or a capex purchase?
Choose the PPA if you want savings without spending capital, prefer someone else to carry the maintenance and performance risk, or want to keep your balance sheet clean. Choose capex if you have capital available and want the maximum lifetime return. You'll own the asset, keep 100% of the power, and can use SEAI grants and Accelerated Capital Allowances. We'll quote both side by side so the numbers make the decision easy.
Does solar actually work in Ireland's climate?
Yes. Solar PV generates from daylight, not direct sunshine, and Ireland gets 1,100–1,400 hours of sunshine a year. A well-designed commercial system in Ireland typically offsets 30–70% of daytime electricity demand. Panel efficiency actually benefits from our cooler temperatures.
How long does it take to get up and running?
From initial survey to go-live is typically 6–10 weeks for most commercial sites, including design, ESB Networks notification, and planning where required. The on-site installation itself usually takes just 1–3 days, with minimal disruption to your operations.
Do I need planning permission?
Most rooftop commercial solar in Ireland is exempt from planning permission under the Planning and Development Regulations, provided the system meets size and siting criteria. Where planning is needed, we manage the entire process on your behalf as part of the project.
What if I don't own the building?
PPAs work for tenants too. We'll need your landlord's consent for the roof, and we regularly structure agreements that work for both parties (landlords often welcome the upgrade, since it adds value to the building). Tell us your situation in the quote form and we'll advise on the best structure.
What happens if I sell the building or the business?
The PPA can transfer to the new owner or occupier. A below-market electricity rate is an asset that typically makes the property more attractive, not less. Alternatively, you can buy out the system as part of the sale. We'll work with you to find the cleanest outcome.
Can excess electricity be sold back to the grid?
Yes. Under Ireland's Clean Export Guarantee, surplus generation can be exported and paid for. That said, we design systems to maximise self-consumption first, because using your own solar power is worth far more than exporting it.
What maintenance is required?
Very little. Rain keeps panels clean in the Irish climate, and there are no moving parts. Under the PPA it's all handled by us anyway: remote monitoring, annual inspections, inverter care and any repairs. Capex customers can add our O&M package for the same peace of mind.
Who funds the system, and what if Aura Energy isn't around in year 15?
Fair question, and one every business should ask a PPA provider. Each agreement is structured so the system and the contract can transfer to another qualified operator if ever required, and your rate and terms are protected in the contract itself, not dependent on any one company. The panels are on your roof either way: they keep generating regardless. We'll happily walk your solicitor or accountant through the agreement before you sign.
My roof is 20 years old. Can it still take solar?
Often, yes. Our free survey includes a structural and condition check, and we won't put panels on a roof that isn't fit to carry them for the term. If cladding or membrane work is due in the next few years, it usually makes sense to do that first, and we can coordinate with your roofing contractor. If repairs are ever needed mid-term, the agreement covers lifting and reinstating the panels, so you're never locked out of maintaining your own building.
Will we get stuck in a grid connection queue?
For typical commercial rooftop systems, no. The multi-year queues you read about apply to large utility-scale projects. Rooftop systems at this scale go through ESB Networks' standard notification and application processes, which we submit at the very start of your project so the paperwork runs in parallel with design and procurement. Timelines vary by system size and site, and we'll give you a realistic programme up front.
What if our electricity use falls, or the site is quiet for part of the year?
We size the system against your actual consumption data, deliberately conservatively, so the roof serves your dependable base load rather than your best month. Any surplus can still be exported under the Clean Export Guarantee. And if your circumstances change materially, say you downsize or relocate, the agreement includes practical options: transfer to a new occupier, early buyout, or restructuring. We'd rather design for reality than argue about it later.
