Modern slavery and supply chain statement

Last updated: 30 October 2026

This is a voluntary statement about slavery, forced labour and human trafficking in our business and supply chain. Aura Energy is below the turnover thresholds that make such statements mandatory, but solar hardware has a documented forced-labour risk in parts of its global supply chain, and our customers are entitled to know how we deal with that. This statement covers the financial year ending [31 December 2026].

Our business

Aura Energy Limited is a Dublin-based commercial solar company. We fund, design, install, own and operate rooftop solar on Irish business premises under power purchase agreements, and we design and build systems that businesses buy outright. Installation is carried out by vetted Irish subcontractors under our design and supervision.

Our supply chain

The hardware we buy is mainly solar panels, inverters, mounting systems and electrical components, purchased from established distributors supplying recognised tier-1 manufacturers. The best documented risk in this chain is forced labour in the production of polysilicon, the raw material of most solar cells, and in some upstream wafer and cell manufacturing.

What we do about it

  • We buy tier-1 panels and inverters through established European distribution channels, not unverified traders.
  • We ask panel suppliers for supply-chain transparency documentation tracing the origin of the polysilicon, wafers and cells in what we buy, and we keep what they provide.
  • We prefer manufacturers that take part in recognised industry supply-chain initiatives, such as the Solar Stewardship Initiative, or that publish independent supply-chain audits.
  • As supplier contracts are put in place, we include terms prohibiting forced labour and allowing us to end the relationship if a supplier cannot show its chain is clean.
  • From 14 December 2027 the EU's Forced Labour Regulation bans placing products made with forced labour on the EU market. We are building our purchasing records so we can meet it.
  • Our installation subcontractors are Irish-registered businesses, insured and subject to Irish employment law, and we communicate these expectations to them.

We are an early-stage company, and these controls will deepen as we grow. What will not change is the principle: if a supplier cannot show us where its materials come from, we buy elsewhere.

Raising a concern

Anyone, inside or outside the company, can raise a concern about slavery or forced labour in our business or supply chain at info@auraenergy.ie. We will look into every report.

Review

We review this statement every year. Next review: October 2027.

Approved by Martin Molloy, Director, Aura Energy Limited, 30 October 2026.
