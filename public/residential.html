Why go solar

What solar does for your home

Smaller bills

Every unit your roof makes is one you don't buy. A battery or diverter lets you use more of it yourself.

A price that doesn't rise

The power from your roof costs the same in year ten as it does on day one, whatever happens to energy prices.

A better BER

Solar lifts your home's Building Energy Rating, which buyers look at and green mortgages are based on.

Paid for what you export

Spare power flows to the grid, and your electricity supplier pays you for it under the Clean Export Guarantee.

Common questions

Quick answers before you go solar

The questions Irish homeowners ask most. Don't see yours? Give us a call.

How much does solar cost for my home?
It depends on your roof, how many panels suit it and whether you add a battery. Your online estimate gives you a guide in about a minute, and the free survey gives you a fixed written price.
How much is the SEAI grant, and do I qualify?
Up to €1,800 for solar panels, if your home was built and lived in before 2025. The grant must be approved before any work starts, and your home needs a BER once the panels are fitted. We'll walk you through it.
Does solar work in Irish weather?
Yes. Panels run on daylight, not heat, so they keep working on cloudy days. They make most of their power from April to September and less in winter, and your estimate allows for that.
Is my roof suitable?
South-facing roofs make the most power, but east and west roofs work well too and spread it across the day. Flat roofs work with tilted frames. North-facing or heavily shaded roofs suit solar less, and we'll tell you honestly at your survey.
Is a battery worth it?
A battery stores the spare power your panels make during the day, so you can use it in the evening when you need it most. It can also charge on cheaper night rates. A new €600 SEAI battery grant may also apply, and we'll confirm it at your survey.
Can I sell the power I don't use?
Yes. Under the Clean Export Guarantee, your electricity supplier pays you for the power you send to the grid, and rates vary by supplier. We register your system with ESB Networks, and you'll need a smart meter, which ESB Networks fits if you don't have one.
Do I need planning permission?
Most homes don't. Since 2022, solar panels on house roofs are exempt from planning permission in most cases. Protected structures, some conservation areas and areas near airports can have limits, and we check this at your survey.
How long does fitting take, and what upkeep is needed?
Fitting usually takes about a day. After that there's very little to do: rain keeps the panels clean, and they're built to last 25 years or more. The inverter, which turns your panels' power into power your home can use, usually needs replacing once in that time.
